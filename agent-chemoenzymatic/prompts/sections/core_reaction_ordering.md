## Reaction ordering strategy - forward cost governs retrosynthetic priority

A retrosynthetic order is the reverse of a forward synthesis. Choosing *when* to form each bond in the forward direction is as important as *which* bond to break in retrosynthesis. Follow these priorities:

### Priority 1: Expensive / scarce building blocks -> disconnect early in retrosynthesis (form late in forward)

If a fragment is costly (e.g. doxorubicin hydrochloride), disconnect it as early as possible - that is, close to the target in the retrosynthetic tree. This means it enters the forward synthesis at the very last step, minimising material loss across multiple transformations.

### Priority 2: Sterically-hindered / yield-sensitive couplings -> disconnect late in retrosynthesis (perform early in forward)

Reactions whose yield degrades with substrate size (e.g. Suzuki coupling on large, crowded scaffolds) should be performed when the substrate is still small. In retrosynthetic terms, these bonds should be broken *late* - close to the building-block end of the tree - so that in the forward direction the coupling happens on small, high-yielding fragments.

### Priority 3: Labile / unstable motifs -> disconnect early in retrosynthesis (install late in forward)

Phosphate esters, sensitive protecting groups, and other motifs prone to cleavage under downstream conditions should be introduced at the final stages of forward synthesis. In retrosynthesis: break these bonds early, near the target.

### Priority 4: Poorly-soluble intermediates -> resolve early

Multi-carboxylate or highly polar intermediates that cause handling difficulties should be disconnected or partitioned onto separate branches early in the retrosynthetic tree, so the forward route never accumulates multiple difficult solubility motifs in the same step.

### Mnemonic

> **Expensive -> early cut . Sterically hindered -> late cut . Labile -> early cut . Hard-to-handle -> early cut**
